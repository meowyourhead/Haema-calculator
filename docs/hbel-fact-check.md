# HBEL comments — fact-check notes (for later review)

Prepared 26 Nov 2026 while building the HBEL tab. **Nothing below has been changed in
the app**: comments and criteria are kept as written until the lab reviews these points.
Codes refer to the HBEL tab numbering (sheet rows 29 and 31 = codes 25 and 26).
Please verify against current references and the analyser's (Bio-Rad) documentation
before changing any LabWare comment.

## Likely errors

| # | Code | Issue | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 7, 11, 12 (homozygous), 14, 24 | Comment starts "HbA present" | HbA is absent in β⁰-thalassaemia major, HbSC disease, homozygous HPFH (HbF ~100 %), HbEE and HbE/β⁰-thalassaemia (unless transfused). Only β⁺ genotypes retain some HbA. |
| 2 | 11 | "Suspected Hb SC trait" | HbS + HbC (no HbA) is HbSC **disease**, a compound heterozygous state, not a trait. |
| 3 | 10 | Criterion "Presence of sickle cell on smear" for HbS trait | Sickle cells are normally not seen on the film in sickle cell trait; they are a feature of HbSS / HbSC. Using it as a criterion would steer away from the trait comment. |
| 4 | 7 | HbA2 "Absent to Low" for β-thal major | HbA2 in β-thal major is variable (often normal or raised as a proportion); "absent" is not expected. Transfusion also alters all fractions. |
| 5 | 21 | Low Hb + low indices + raised HbF → "Suspected HPFH" | HPFH usually has normal red cell indices. Microcytosis with raised HbF and normal HbA2 is more typical of δβ-thalassaemia trait (or co-inherited α-/β-thal, iron deficiency). |
| 6 | 25 | Comment says "HbF minimally elevated" but criterion says HbF "Normal" | Internal inconsistency in the sheet. |

## Worth reviewing

| # | Code | Point |
|---|---|---|
| 7 | 9 | "Microcytes/target cells/spherocytes": target cells fit HbC trait; spherocytes / microspherocytes and HbC crystals are more a feature of HbCC. |
| 8 | 5, 6 | HbA2 "4–8 %": β-thal trait is usually ~3.5–7 %. Values >7–8 % should raise suspicion of a co-eluting variant. Also the gap between "4–8" and ">10". |
| 9 | 7 | HbF "92–95 %" fits β⁰/β⁰; in β⁺ major HbF varies widely (~30–90 %). |
| 10 | 12 | Heterozygous HPFH: HbA2 is typically low-normal rather than "absent". Homozygous HPFH has mild microcytosis / erythrocytosis rather than strictly "normal" indices. |
| 11 | 24 | HbE/β-thal: HbF commonly 30–60 %; "10–40 %" may be narrow. |
| 12 | 4 | Occasional HbH inclusions fit α⁰-trait (--/αα); numerous inclusions suggest HbH disease (check for an HbH peak). Negative inclusions do not exclude α-thalassaemia. |
| 13 | 15 | Falsely raised HbF from high HbA1c (and from other modified Hb, e.g. carbamylated or acetylated) is described on some HPLC systems; confirm for your analyser/program. Repeating after 3 months only helps if glycaemic control changes. |
| 14 | 22 | With a δ-chain variant, true HbA2 = HbA2 + HbA2′; not summing them can mask β-thal trait. The comment avoids reporting a value, which is safe. |
| 15 | 16, 20 | Reference range is 2.2–3.4 %, borderline 3.5–3.9 %; values like 3.45 need a rounding rule. Borderline HbA2 can reflect mild β⁺ alleles or iron deficiency masking β-trait. |
| 16 | all | Spelling is mixed: "haemoglobin" / "hemoglobin", "Thalassemia" (US) / "Haemoglobin" (UK). Pick one style. |
